Method

How the crew
is decided.

Hard constraints first, fairness second, and a standby pass at the end. Nothing in this page is a black box, and nothing here is a promise the evaluation does not support.

Solver OR-Tools CP-SAT Version 9.15.6755

Five moves, in order

constraints before preferences
  1. Read the brief. Roles are proposed with a source phrase and a confidence label. Every open question is surfaced as a decision, not silently answered.
  2. Confirm the roles. The organiser sets the count and the dialect. Safety roles are marked before the solver runs, so the gate is never relaxed to make a roster look full.
  3. Filter to eligible. Available in the slot, inside the travel limit, skill level at or above the minimum. Minors only where the brief allows them and guardian consent is on file.
  4. Optimise in tiers. Fill every essential slot, then maximise dropout robustness, then spread turns. Ties break on distance and fit, never on seniority alone.
  5. Add the bench. A second pass names a standby for each essential role. If it cannot, the drive is not ready and the gap is printed.

Order matters: a fairness score never overrides an unfilled first-aid slot.

Allowed to do
  • Fill a slot only with someone free in that slot.
  • Respect the travel limit on the brief.
  • Enforce the minimum skill level.
  • Require a host check for safety roles.
  • Use a minor only if allowed and consented.
Not allowed to do
  • Guess a missing count and hide it.
  • Relax a safety gate to fill a slot.
  • Confirm a contribution card.
  • Send anything to a volunteer.
  • Claim the chain proves truth.
The one-line version Hard constraints decide who is eligible. Fairness only ranks the eligible. A human confirms every card, and the log records what changed.

Five metrics, in plain words

definitions & formulas
Role coverage
Did every essential slot get a person?
The share of must-have slots that were filled. A “should” role does not count toward the verdict. 0.9167 across ten drives means 91.67% of essential slots were filled.
filled essential slots ÷ required essential slots
Dropout robustness
What happens if someone cannot come?
How often removing any single person still leaves every essential role covered. 1.0 means every single dropout has a named replacement. 0 means the first absence opens a hole.
single-person dropouts covered ÷ single-person dropouts tested
Repeat-pick share
Are we going back to the same faces?
The share of placements given to someone already picked in the last 90 days. Lower is better. The planner records 0.23 across the sample; manual greedy records 0.4267.
picks of people picked in the last 90 days ÷ all picks
Exposure Gini
How unevenly are turns spread?
A 0–1 number borrowed from economics. 0 means everyone got the same number of turns; 1 means one person took everything. It is only ever shown here and on the evaluation page, with this sentence beside it.
Gini over per-volunteer pick counts
Drives ready
Can the organiser actually run it?
The share of drives that come back READY: every essential role filled and any one dropout covered. This is the number a district feels, so it leads the evaluation page.
drives with a READY verdict ÷ all drives
What no metric captures
The part a number cannot hold
Whether a young person felt useful, whether the village trusted the camp, whether the host would invite the same crew again. Those are not scored here.

Honest limits

read before trusting a verdict
  1. A hash chain shows edits, not truth. It proves a record was not quietly rewritten. It cannot prove the hours were worked. Truth comes from host confirmation.
  2. Ten tasks is a small sample. The robustness interval runs from 0.50 to 0.96. The direction of the result is clear; the exact size is not.
  3. “Not solved” is not “impossible”. Eight drives in this capture were never run through a roster. Two that were run could not be filled with the people available.
  4. Every person is synthetic. No real volunteer, family or minor appears in this data set. It is a design instrument, not a live register.
  5. The safety gate is only as good as the host check. The rule refuses an unchecked skill; it cannot make a check happen.
  6. The extractor is not authoritative. It returned four open questions on the Hindi brief and labelled one role low confidence. A person resolves them.
  7. No integration, no auto-send. SewaCircle complements MY Bharat and does not connect to it. Nothing is posted, messaged or filed automatically.
