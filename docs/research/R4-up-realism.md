## Findings

**District reference points (FACT).** `docs/research/up_districts.json` contains exactly 20 real Uttar Pradesh district names with one WGS84 point each, copied verbatim from the `geo.lat`/`geo.lon` fields of the Open Admin Data `all-district.json` file that was opened and parsed on 2026-11-18: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/open-admin-data/india-administrative-divisions/master/data/all-district.json (repository page: https://github.com/open-admin-data/india-administrative-divisions ; metadata there says CC-BY-4.0, last updated 2026-08-20). Each JSON row carries the same shared `source` URL plus a per-district `source_record_id` (for example Lucknow `IN19D003`, Kanpur Dehat `IN19D004`, Varanasi `IN19D019`, Gorakhpur `IN19D026`), so the citation is auditable row by row. **Caveat (FACT about the source):** the dataset publishes one representative point per district but does not document it as a polygon centroid; these are approximate district locator points, not surveyed centres or legal boundaries. The source lists 69 UP districts, whereas Wikipedia lists 75 districts in 18 divisions (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uttar_Pradesh, published 2026-11-01), so our 20-district file is a deliberately incomplete demo slice. Names follow the source verbatim, including `Allahabad` (present-day Prayagraj is called the judicial capital on the same Wikipedia page).

**Real sub-district units for the four focus districts (FACT).**
- Kanpur Dehat: the district site states 6 tehsils and 10 development blocks (https://kanpurdehat.nic.in/organisation-chart/ ). Tehsils: Akbarpur, Bhognipur, Derapur, Rasulabad, Sikandara, Maitha (https://kanpurdehat.nic.in/tehsil/ ). Blocks: Akbarpur, Maitha, Sarvankheda, Derapur, Jhinjhak, Rasulabad, Amraudha, Malasa, Sandalpur, Rajpur (https://kanpurdehat.nic.in/subdivision-blocks/ ).
- Lucknow: the Chief Development Officer's page lists 8 development blocks with BDOs — BKT, Chinhat, Gosaiganj, Kakori, Mall, Malihabad, Mohanlalganj, Sarojini Nagar (https://lucknow.nic.in/details-of-officers-posted-in-district-development-block/ ). The district's Hindi tehsil page says there are five tehsils and names them Sadar (सदर), Sarojini Nagar, Bakhshi Ka Talab, Malihabad, Mohanlalganj (https://lucknow.nic.in/hi/%E0%A4%A4%E0%A4%B9%E0%A4%B8%E0%A5%80%E0%A4%B2-%E0%A4%95%E0%A4%BE-%E0%A4%A8%E0%A4%BE%E0%A4%AE/ , page footer "पिछला संशोधन: 27.01.2026").
- Varanasi: the district administration page states 3 sub-divisions and 8 development blocks (https://varanasi.nic.in/administrative-setup/ ); the DEO page names 3 tehsils and two blocks, Kashi Vidyapeeth and Cholapur (https://varanasi.nic.in/deo-portal/ ). The district's Hindi education page lists block-level education officers at Cholapur, Harhua, Pindra, Badagaon, Chiraigaon, Sevapuri, Arajiline, Kashi Vidyapeeth and others (https://varanasi.nic.in/hi/%E0%A4%B6%E0%A4%BF%E0%A4%95%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%B7%E0%A4%BE/ ). These are block names as printed on an official district page, not a certified exhaustive block list.
- Gorakhpur: the district page states 7 tehsils, 19 blocks, 191 Nyay Panchayats and 1354 Gram Sabhas, and maps tehsils to blocks, e.g. Sadar → Jungle Kaudia (partial), Chargawa, Bhathat, Khorabar, Pipraich; Bansgaon → Bansgaon, Kauriram, Gagaha; Gola → Gola, Barhalganj, Uruwa (https://gorakhpur.nic.in/subdivisions-blocks/ ). A separate official blocks page lists Campierganj, Jungle Kaudia, Khajni, Belghat, Sardarnagar, Brahmpur, Charganwa, Bhathat, Khorabar, Pipraich, Sahjanwa, Pali, Piprauli, Bansgaon, Kauriram, Gagaha, Gola, Barhalganj, Uruwa, Bharohiya — 20 rows against the prose count of 19 (https://gorakhpur.nic.in/blocks/ ); this inconsistency is flagged rather than resolved.

**Youth-organisation structure (FACT).** The Ministry of Youth Affairs and Sports Annual Report 2013-14 (https://yas.nic.in/sites/default/files/Annual%20Report13-14.pdf ) records: NSS is implemented through institutions; each NSS unit is about 100 student volunteers led by a teacher designated Programme Officer, and each unit adopts a village or slum; universities have an NSS Cell and Programme Coordinator, states a State NSS Cell headed by a State Liaison Officer, and NSS Headquarters works through 15 Regional Centres including Lucknow. The same report says NYKS activities are carried out through a District Youth Coordinator in each district and 2 National Youth Corps volunteers in each block, with 28 zonal offices. A live MY Bharat event page shows the district-level organiser naming pattern: "MERA YUVA BHARAT PILIBHIT" as event organiser for a Pilibhit, Uttar Pradesh campaign (https://mybharat.gov.in/vo/pages/event_detail?event_name=Seva-Sankalp-Abhiyan-aewam-Seva-Pakhwara&key=510216192510 ). Taken together, the realistic organiser chain for our demo is: district youth office / NYK coordinator → block-level youth club or NSS unit / Programme Officer → volunteer roster.

**Volunteer age band (FACT).** MY Bharat is described by Digital India as a one-stop portal for people aged 15 to 29, available in 15 Indian languages (https://www.digitalindia.gov.in/initiative/my-bharat/ ). A Government of India National e-Governance Division press note on NSS says students aged 15–29 across schools, colleges and universities can participate (https://negd.gov.in/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/PR_Ep-51_NSS_V1.pdf ). **INFERENCE:** no opened source gives a measured age histogram, so any within-band skew (for example more 18–24-year-olds because NSS is college-based) is our modelling choice, not a cited statistic.

**Language mix (FACT + INFERENCE).** Census 2011 Table C-16 for Uttar Pradesh documents mother-tongue categories including Hindi, Awadhi, Bhojpuri, Brajbhasha, Bundeli, Bagheli, Urdu and English, at state/district/tehsil granularity (https://censusindia.gov.in/nada/index.php/catalog/10224 ). The opened Wikipedia Uttar Pradesh page says Hindi is the most widely spoken language, Bhojpuri is used by nearly 11% of the population, Urdu about 5.4%, Punjabi 0.3% and Bengali 0.1% (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uttar_Pradesh ). The opened "Languages of Uttar Pradesh" page names Awadhi, Bagheli, Bhojpuri, Braj, Bundeli, Kannauji and Kauravi as regional languages, and notes Bhojpuri is spoken across eastern districts including Varanasi, Gorakhpur, Ballia, Deoria, Kushinagar, Chandauli, Mau and Azamgarh (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languages_of_Uttar_Pradesh ). **INFERENCE:** no opened source supplies a volunteer-population language histogram; a demographic-style mix (Hindi-dominant, Bhojpuri-heavier in eastern districts, Awadhi in Awadh, Urdu in urban pockets, some Hindi/English code-mixing) is a design assumption to label as such.

**Real community-drive kinds (FACT).** A MY Bharat district campaign in Pilibhit lists cleanliness and sanitation drives, shramdaan, environmental awareness, plantation, water conservation, plastic-free initiatives, public awareness campaigns, community outreach, youth dialogues and social service, with documentary records and photographs (https://mybharat.gov.in/vo/pages/event_detail?event_name=Seva-Sankalp-Abhiyan-aewam-Seva-Pakhwara&key=510216192510 ). An NSS 7-day special camp listing describes door-to-door socio-economic and health surveys, shramdaan in public spaces and water bodies, health and digital-literacy awareness rallies, plantation, welfare-scheme awareness and cultural sessions (https://mybharat.gov.in/elp/listing/158448 ). The YAS Annual Report 2013-14 lists NSS core activity categories: adult literacy, pre-school and continuing education; immunisation, blood donation, health education and AIDS awareness; plantation, street and drain cleaning; work in hospitals and institutions for disabled people, orphanages and old-age homes (https://yas.nic.in/sites/default/files/Annual%20Report13-14.pdf ). A MY Bharat disaster event with an NSS unit and NDRF resource team covers disaster preparedness, search and rescue, first aid, evacuation, community awareness and supporting relief operations (https://mybharat.gov.in/vo/pages/event_detail?event_name=Disaster&key=115214641240 ). These are the real drive archetypes our synthetic tasks should imitate.

**Skill categories observed on official youth platforms (FACT).** MY Bharat public youth profiles expose user-entered skill strings and areas of interest; opened examples include EVENT PLANNING SKILLS, LEADERSHIP SKILLS, NEGOTIATION SKILLS, PROBLEM-SOLVING SKILLS, PROJECT COORDINATION SKILLS, PROJECT MANAGEMENT SKILLS, PUBLIC RELATIONS SKILLS, ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE AND MACHINE LEARNING SKILLS, ANALYTICAL SKILLS, COMMUNICATION SKILLS, COACHING AND MENTORING SKILLS, CONFLICT RESOLUTION SKILLS, CREATIVITY, WRITING SKILLS, ADAPTABILITY/FLEXIBILITY, FINANCIAL MANAGEMENT SKILLS and BUSINESS DEVELOPMENT SKILLS, with interests such as Technology and Science, Arts and Culture, Education and Learning, Media & PR Communications, Production & Events, Sports and Fitness and Entrepreneurship and Business (https://mybharat.gov.in/ypp/youth-public-profile/ , https://mybharat.gov.in/ypp/youth-public-profile/details/rajat48360 ). An NSS experiential-learning listing gives ELP domains Cyber Security, Leadership Development, Public Relations, Road Safety and Seva Se Seekhen (https://mybharat.gov.in/elp/listing/158448 ). The NeGD press note names leadership, confidence, teamwork, communication, crisis management, photography and video editing as capabilities built through NSS (https://negd.gov.in/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/PR_Ep-51_NSS_V1.pdf ). **INFERENCE:** MY Bharat displays user-entered strings, not a controlled official taxonomy; the 24-skill list below is our proposed normalisation, not a government standard.

## Recommendation for our build

- Load `docs/research/up_districts.json` as the geography registry. Use the 20 points for haversine travel buckets (for example <15 km, 15–50 km, >50 km) and for the district-spread fairness metric, but print "approximate district point" in any UI tooltip; never imply legal boundaries or precise centroids. Keep the file's `synthetic_data_label` visible in the demo data panel and in exported CSV/JSON.
- Seed demo tasks only with block/tehsil names verified above: Kanpur Dehat (Akbarpur, Bhognipur, Derapur, Rasulabad, Sikandara, Maitha; blocks Akbarpur, Maitha, Sarvankheda, Jhinjhak, Rasulabad, Amraudha, Malasa, Sandalpur, Rajpur), Lucknow (BKT, Chinhat, Gosaiganj, Kakori, Mall, Malihabad, Mohanlalganj, Sarojini Nagar; tehsils Sadar, Sarojini Nagar, Bakhshi Ka Talab, Malihabad, Mohanlalganj), Varanasi (Kashi Vidyapeeth, Cholapur, Arajiline, Chiraigaon, Harhua, Pindra, Sevapuri, Badagaon), Gorakhpur (Pipraich, Khorabar, Bhathat, Chargawa, Sardarnagar, Bansgaon, Gola, Barhalganj and the other official block names). Skip Bharohiya in the demo until the 19-vs-20 discrepancy is resolved.
- Generate every volunteer record with: synthetic name, age 15–29 (INFERENCE distribution), district + block from the verified lists, languages from the Census categories, availability windows, and 3–6 skills drawn from the 24-skill taxonomy below. All names must be invented; do not copy names, phone numbers or emails from the official pages or MY Bharat profiles.

## Risks

- **Data-quality risk:** Open Admin Data is a community-maintained dataset (69 UP rows) with no documented centroid method; the 20 points are demo locator points only. The source also uses older names (`Allahabad`, `Kansiram Nagar`). Do not use them for real navigation, eligibility or eligibility-radius decisions.
- **Official-page drift:** district websites change, and the Gorakhpur block count in the opened prose (19) conflicts with the opened table (20 rows). If a demo card needs an exact block count, quote the prose count and show the inconsistency rather than silently picking one.
- **Contact information:** the opened district and MY Bharat pages contain real officials' phone numbers and emails. Copying them into synthetic fixtures would mix real people into a "synthetic" demo; we copied only geographic/administrative names and no contact details.
- **Taxonomy risk:** MY Bharat skill strings are user-entered and come with an on-page disclaimer; they are not an official controlled vocabulary. Our 24-skill table is a proposal (INFERENCE), and 4 rows are explicitly inferred. Do not present it as a government skill framework.
- **Distribution risk:** no opened source measured within-15–29 age shares or a volunteer language histogram. Any percentages used to generate volunteers must be printed as INFERENCE in the eval README; presenting them as census or MY Bharat statistics would be a false claim.
- **Synthetic-data integrity:** every volunteer, task, host note and contribution card must remain labelled SYNTHETIC, with no real names, no real minors and no reuse of real profile text from public youth listings.
- **Date sensitivity:** some opened pages are 2026 event pages and press notes; treat them as evidence that a drive type exists, not as a claim that the event is ongoing or that its dates are current.
